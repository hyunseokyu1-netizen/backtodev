# A Developer's Place in the AI Era (2/5): FDE, AI Native Engineer, and GTM Owner Aren't on the Same Floor

In [Part 1](/posts/ai_era_developer_01_dogfooding_20260928), I wrote that what gets replaced is implementing what you're told, and what's left is deciding what needs implementing.

Companies call that leftover work by several names. FDE, AI Native Engineer, GTM Owner. A lot of posts line these three up and introduce them as "hot job categories right now" — but **looked at against the actual job market, these three aren't on the same floor.**

One has already hardened into an actual job. One is scattered across several names. One isn't even a job title yet — it's still a role concept. If you don't separate these, you'll prepare in the wrong direction.

---

## 1. The Landscape in One Table

As of 2026, here's where I see these three categories sitting.

| Category | Trend strength | Job-title standardization | AI relevance | Core thing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **FDE** | 🔥🔥🔥 Very strong | **Rising** | Very high | Actually deploying AI on-site with customers |
| **AI Native Engineer** | 🔥🔥🔥 Strong | **Still low** | Very high | Building and automating with AI |
| **GTM Owner** | 🔥🔥 Strong | **Low** | High | Owning go-to-market and revenue for AI products |

All three are genuinely job categories that emerged in the AI era. But **it's hard to call them equally "trending."** The key is that the standardization column is completely different for each.

---

## 2. FDE — The Only Name That's Hardened Into a Job

Of the three, this is the only one you can call an actual "job."

Reuters covered [what's the hottest job in AI right now](https://www.reuters.com/) on February 26, 2026, and the answer was FDE. You can't base a judgment on a single article, but the hiring data points the same direction.

| Metric | Number |
|---|---|
| FDE job postings on Indeed (April 2025) | 643 |
| FDE job postings on Indeed (April 2026) | **5,330** |
| Year-over-year increase | **~729%** |
| Lightcast's reported increase | **over 1,000%** |

Going from hundreds to thousands in a single year. **A curve like that isn't explained by the word "trend."**

And there's a more important signal. Companies like OpenAI, Anthropic, Databricks, and Palantir are hiring FDEs **as a dedicated org and job category.** That means they're not just bolting requirements onto an existing role — they built a separate organization for it. That's real evidence of job standardization.

Another piece of evidence for standardization: line up several job postings and **the requirements look alike.** When different companies want nearly the same thing, the market has already converged on an understanding of the role.

### FDE Itself Is Evolving Toward AI

This is the most interesting part. FDE wasn't originally an AI job category. It was an engineer who went on-site at customer companies and built systems. The content of the role changed.

```
The old FDE
Customer company → gather requirements → build system → deploy

FDE today
Customer problem → AI Agent / RAG / LLM → integrate with existing systems
          → automate the actual workflow → get it to stick with the customer
```

There are two differences. **The front end shifted from "requirements" to "problem," and the back end shifted from "deploy" to "stick."**

Instead of receiving requirements, you find the problem. Instead of stopping at deployment, you get it to a state where it's actually used. As I wrote in [the post dedicated to the FDE role](/posts/fde_forward_deployed_engineer_20260923), this is a role where building something nobody uses counts as failure.

Actual job postings confirm this. Databricks' [AI FDE job posting](https://www.databricks.com/company/careers/professional-services-operations/ai-engineer---fde-forward-deployed-engineer-8546367002) describes the role as building GenAI applications in production environments while working directly with customers, and lists RAG, multi-agent systems, Text2SQL, and fine-tuning as required experience.

**Customer-facing work and LLM operations are now attached to a single person.** Those used to be two different jobs.

## 3. AI Native Engineer — It's Not a Job Trending, It's Developers Changing

Here's where I need to flip my perspective. I understood this as a new job category at first too, but once I actually went looking at postings, that wasn't it.

**The name "AI Native Engineer" itself isn't standardized the way FDE is.** Every company calls it something different.

| Names companies use |
|---|
| AI Engineer |
| Applied AI Engineer |
| AI Software Engineer |
| AI Engineer, FDE |
| AI Solutions Engineer |
| AI Architect |

If a name is scattered across six variants, it isn't a job yet. So the accurate way to read it is:

> **It's not that "the job of AI Native Engineer is trending" — developers themselves are shifting to an AI-native way of working.**

The evidence is in the requirements side of job postings. In 2026 postings, the share demanding AI-related skills grew fast, and **especially in technical roles, AI fluency is becoming a general requirement rather than a specialized skill.**

What that means matters.

| If it were a new category | If it's the new baseline |
|---|---|
| You apply for that category | **It bleeds into every developer posting** |
| If it doesn't fit, look at another category | There's nowhere to hide |
| Your competitors are applicants to that category | Your competitors are every developer |
| Preparation = study that category | **Preparation = change how you work, period** |

Reality is closer to the right column. So AINE is better understood as **"not a seat you can prepare into," but "the range of what a developer is has shifted."** Things like dev automation, AI-assisted design, and output verification aren't a separate category's specialty anymore — they're sliding down into a developer's basic job description.

One more thing — look at the fourth name on that list: **AI Engineer, FDE.** AINE and FDE sitting inside one job title together. That's evidence the boundary is already blending.

---

## 4. GTM Owner — Not a Job, an Organizational Shift

This one needs more care. **The name "GTM Owner" isn't a standardized single job the way FDE is.** You struggle to even find postings under that exact name.

So why does this concept keep coming up? Because how organizations operate is changing.

```
The old structure — four orgs move separately
Product  →  Engineering  →  Sales  →  Customer Success

The current flow — treated as one
Build the AI product  →  deploy it with the customer  →  spread it in the market
```

As AI companies grew, they realized that handing these three stages off in sequence meant the product doesn't sell. If the builder doesn't understand deployment, and the deployer doesn't understand revenue, it leaks in the middle every time.

So multiple roles are emerging on the GTM side. They're multiplying without a unified name.

| Job names emerging on the GTM side |
|---|
| GTM Lead |
| GTM Engineer |
| Technical GTM |
| Product Marketing |
| Solutions Architect |
| Solutions Engineer |
| AI Transformation Lead |
| Business Development |
| Product / Business Owner |

Nine different names, overlapping work. **The fact that the name hasn't settled yet is the current state of this space.**

But separate from job-posting names, the questions I got in interviews were already pointed this direction. Some I actually got asked:

- How much would it cost to maintain this feature
- Would the customer feel that cost is worth paying
- Should we build this ourselves, or buy it

**None of these are technical questions, and none of them can be answered without knowing the technology.** There's no name yet for the person who sits at that intersection.

And here's the interesting part — **interview questions move before job postings do.** Postings lag because they have to use existing category names; interviewers move fast because they ask for what's actually needed. The demand arrives before the name does.

---

## 5. Three Categories, One Sentence Each

| | |
|---|---|
| **FDE** | **It's a job.** You can apply by that name, and there's a dedicated org for it |
| **AI Native Engineer** | **It's the new baseline for developers.** Not a separate category — it comes down as a requirement |
| **GTM Owner** | **It's a direction organizations are merging toward.** Not a name yet, just a role concept |

Laid out this way, the question changes. **"Which category should I prepare for" is the wrong question.** You don't pick between these — they're on different floors.

A realistic order looks like this:

1. **Being AI-native isn't optional.** It's become the baseline no matter where you end up
2. **FDE has the most open seats right now.** It's the only one you can apply to by name, and it has a clear definition
3. **The GTM side is where you build experience before the name settles.** If you wait for the posting, you're late

Saying FDE is about to become legacy would be an exaggeration. It's the hottest seat right now. **But staying purely as "an FDE and nothing else" probably drops you a layer down** — that part I do believe. A realistic strategy is sitting in the hot seat now while preparing to answer the next layer's questions.

---

## 6. Wrap-up

1. **The three categories are at completely different maturity levels.** Lining them up for comparison leads to a wrong read
2. **Only FDE has hardened into a job.** Postings went from 643 to 5,330 in a year, and big companies hire it as a dedicated org
3. **The content of FDE has shifted toward AI.** Gathering requirements became finding problems; deploying became sticking
4. **AI Native Engineer isn't a job — it's the developer baseline.** The name is scattered across six variants
5. **GTM Owner isn't a job title — it's an organizational shift.** Nine names point at the same work
6. **Interview questions move before job postings do.** Wait for the name and you're late

But looking at these three only as "which one's hotter" misses what actually matters. **AI Native Engineer and GTM Owner aren't competitors — they're opposite directions.** One goes deeper into technology, and the other takes development experience out into business.
